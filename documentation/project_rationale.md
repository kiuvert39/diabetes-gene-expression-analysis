# Project Rationale

## Project Title

**Exploratory Analysis of Gene Expression in Human Pancreatic Islets Associated with Type 2 Diabetes Using R**

## 1. Background

Bioinformatics combines computational methods, programming and statistical techniques to analyse and interpret biological data. With the increasing availability of high-throughput biological datasets, computational approaches have become important in investigating gene activity, understanding biological processes and exploring molecular changes associated with diseases.

Gene-expression analysis provides a means of examining the activity of genes across biological samples. By comparing expression profiles between experimental or clinical groups, researchers can investigate molecular differences associated with particular biological conditions.

Type 2 diabetes is a metabolic disorder involving impaired insulin secretion, insulin action or both. Pancreatic islets contain endocrine cells, including insulin-producing beta cells, which play an essential role in regulating blood glucose. Investigating gene-expression patterns in human pancreatic islets therefore provides an opportunity to explore molecular changes associated with Type 2 diabetes.

## 2. Motivation

With an academic background in Computer Science, this project provides an opportunity to extend programming and computational problem-solving skills into the analysis of biological data.

An interest in applying computing to healthcare and biological research has motivated the exploration of computational approaches for investigating disease-related molecular data. Through this project, practical experience will be developed in R programming, biological data processing, statistical analysis and the interpretation of gene-expression profiles.

The project also represents an initial step towards developing the computational, statistical and biological data-analysis foundations required for further study in Bioinformatics.

## 3. Problem Statement

Gene-expression datasets generated through high-throughput technologies contain measurements across thousands of genes or gene-associated probes and multiple biological samples. Identifying meaningful patterns within such datasets requires appropriate computational methods for data organisation, quality assessment, statistical comparison and biological interpretation.

This project addresses this challenge through an exploratory analysis of publicly available microarray gene-expression data from human pancreatic islets, comparing samples from donors with Type 2 diabetes and non-diabetic donors.

## 4. General Objective

To apply computational and statistical methods using R to explore gene-expression patterns associated with Type 2 diabetes in human pancreatic islets.

## 5. Specific Objectives

- Retrieve and examine a publicly available human pancreatic islet gene-expression dataset.
- Understand the dataset structure, experimental design, sample characteristics and expression measurements.
- Assess data quality and prepare the expression data for appropriate analysis.
- Explore gene-expression patterns and variation across biological samples using statistical summaries and visualisation.
- Compare gene-expression profiles between diabetic and non-diabetic donor groups using appropriate statistical methods.
- Identify candidate differentially expressed genes and investigate their potential biological relevance.
- Interpret the findings while recognising the methodological and biological limitations of the dataset.

## 6. Research Questions

1. What differences in gene-expression profiles can be observed between pancreatic islet samples from donors with Type 2 diabetes and non-diabetic donors?
2. Which genes show statistical evidence of differential expression between the two groups after accounting for multiple testing?
3. How do gene-expression profiles vary across the biological samples, and what patterns can be observed through exploratory visualisation?
4. What biological functions or processes are associated with selected differentially expressed genes?
5. What limitations of the dataset, experimental design and analytical methods should be considered when interpreting the findings?

## 7. Scope of the Project

This project focuses on an introductory computational analysis of publicly available human pancreatic islet microarray data. It will involve data retrieval, inspection, preparation, exploratory analysis, statistical comparison and preliminary biological interpretation using R and relevant bioinformatics tools.

The analysis will focus on the available dataset and its experimental design. It will not attempt to establish causal relationships, identify clinically validated biomarkers, develop a diagnostic system or make medical recommendations.

The findings will be treated as exploratory observations that require further investigation and independent validation.
